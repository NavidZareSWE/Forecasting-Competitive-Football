05 Concepts to study

Ordered by how likely you are to be asked about them, and by how much of your grade rests on the answer.

Proper scoring rules for ordered multiclass outcomes

Why is RPS the primary metric here rather than accuracy or plain Brier?

  • Ranked Probability Score — the cumulative-distribution form, and why it rewards getting the ordering H>D>A right. Draws sit between the two wins, so a confident-but-wrong-side error should cost more than a confident draw error.
  • Brier and log loss — both proper, but log loss is unbounded and punishes a single confident miss arbitrarily hard. Be ready to say which you'd report if you could report only one.
  • What “proper” means: the score is minimised by reporting your true belief. Know why accuracy fails this test.
  • Why the Dummy classifier's rps=0.22812 is the number every other model must beat, and why LightGBM's 0.28461 is a genuine failure rather than noise.

Calibration and reliability

Your model says 40% draw. Over the matches where it says that, do 40% end in draws?

  • Expected Calibration Error — binning, why the bin count is a choice you must justify, and why LightGBM's ece=0.33418 means the probabilities are close to unusable as stated.
  • Isotonic regression — non-parametric, monotone, can overfit small validation sets. Platt / sigmoid scaling — parametric, two parameters, more robust when data is thin. Know the tradeoff, because that is exactly why your code tries isotonic first and falls back.
  • Why calibrate on validation, not train: a model fit on its own training predictions learns to trust itself. And why FrozenEstimator matters — it stops CalibratedClassifierCV from refitting the base model.
  • Reliability diagrams — be able to sketch one and point at where over-confidence shows up.

Class imbalance and synthetic oversampling — P1

Why does the draw class need help, and why does geometry beat linear interpolation?

  • SMOTE baseline: interpolate along the segment to a random k-nearest same-class neighbour. Its failure mode: synthetic points land inside the convex hull only, so it cannot expand the minority region.
  • Geometric SMOTE: generate inside a hypersphere around the seed, then apply truncation (bias toward the neighbour direction) and deformation (collapse the sphere toward a line/plane). Understand what truncation=1 and deformation=1 each degenerate to — SMOTE is a special case.
  • SMOTE-NC / G-SMOTENC: nominal features can't be interpolated. Know the rule your implementation uses (majority vote among neighbours) and why a fractional snapshot_minute is evidence a “continuous” declaration was wrong.
  • Algorithms 1–3 of Fonseca & Bacao (2023), Expert Systems With Applications 234:121053 — the PDF is in docs/papers/. You should be able to derive the continuous-generation step on a whiteboard.
  • Why resampling happens after the train-only fit and never touches the feature CSVs. This is the leakage answer, and it's the one they'll probe.

Temporal validation and leakage

A test-set match's form window draws on training-period matches. Is that leakage?

  • No — those matches concluded before kick-off, which is exactly the information a forecaster would have. Be able to state this crisply; it's a favourite trick question.
  • Why whole dates, not rows, are assigned to splits: incomplete kick-off times mean same-day fixtures could otherwise straddle a boundary. Train ends 2016-01-31, validation 2016-02-01 → 03-18, test 2016-03-19 → 05-17, zero overlap.
  • Why .shift(1) must precede .rolling(5) and not the reverse. Know what the assertion on each team's chronologically-first match proves.
  • The prefix property: np.maximum.accumulate over minute in index order gives a non-decreasing sequence, so searchsorted(..., side="right") returns a contiguous index-ordered prefix. Both directions must be asserted — nothing after t included, nothing at-or-before t excluded — and prefix lengths must be monotone in t.
  • Grouped splitting: 19 snapshots per match are highly correlated. Know why they must never straddle a boundary, and why that makes standard k-fold CV inapplicable.
  • Not a leak, but pre-empt it: at t=90, inplay_goal_diff nearly determines label_margin. That is the task definition. Near-certainty at that horizon is correct behaviour — have the framing ready.

Market probabilities and the betting baseline

Why is the bookmaker so hard to beat, and what does de-vigging actually assume?

  • Overround / vig: Σ 1/oᵢ > 1. Your code asserts this before normalising — know what a violation would mean (arbitrage, or a data error).
  • Proportional de-vigging pᵢ = (1/oᵢ) / Σ(1/oⱼ) assumes the margin is spread evenly across outcomes. It isn't, in reality — favourite-longshot bias means the margin loads onto longshots. Alternatives worth naming: Shin's method, power/odds-ratio methods.
  • Market efficiency: closing odds aggregate information you don't have (team news, sharp money). Beating them on RPS across a full season is a strong claim; frame your result honestly against it.
  • The either/or rule: odds as baseline or odds as feature, never both in one experiment. Know why using both makes the comparison meaningless.
  • Record linkage without a shared key: accent stripping, token similarity, Levenshtein, and why "Ath Madrid" needed a manual override — no string metric separates Athletic from Atlético. The bijection assertion is what makes the join defensible.

The model zoo — what each family assumes

Why did Random Forest beat the boosters, and why is that not embarrassing?

  • Kernel methods: RBF kernel, the C/γ tradeoff, and why SVC(probability=True) is itself Platt scaling under the hood (so you're calibrating a calibrated model — be ready for that). Kernel ridge is O(n²) memory: 17k rows → 2.32 GB.
  • Tree ensembles: bagging (RF, variance reduction, decorrelated trees) vs boosting (sequential bias reduction). Why boosters overfit sooner on ~900 training rows with weak signal, and why HistGradientBoosting bins features.
  • Why the Dummy matters: strategy="prior" for classification, strategy="mean" for regression. A model that loses to it has learned nothing. Reporting that honestly reads as rigour.
  • Regression clipping to [−5, +5] at predict time, and how MAE plus correlation together tell a different story than either alone (Dummy: mae=1.48997, corr=0.0).

Interpretation and ablation

Which features carry the prediction, and does the answer change during a match?

  • SHAP: the Shapley-value foundation, why TreeSHAP is exact and fast for tree ensembles while KernelSHAP is an approximation, and why global bar plots hide interactions that dependence plots reveal.
  • SHAP under correlated features — your home/away/diff columns are strongly correlated by construction. Credit gets split arbitrarily between them. Know this before someone points it out.
  • Ablation design: drop one feature block at a time, retrain, report the delta on the same metric. Keep the split and the seed fixed so the delta is attributable.
  • Error analysis by segment: by league, by snapshot minute, by true outcome. The draw class is where every model bleeds — that's your most interesting finding.
