2Pre-match features — 114 columns per match

Most features come as home_*, away_*, diff_* triplets. Nothing raw enters the model; every column is engineered or extracted.

The leakage rule that governs everything: rolling windows are .shift(1) before .rolling(), ratings are recorded before each match updates them, and every rating snapshot joins with merge_asof strictly dated before kickoff — all enforced by asserts that fail the build, plus 24 hand-computed unit tests.

For scale: the best published academic result on comparable data (CatBoost + pi-ratings, Soccer Prediction Challenge line of work) sits around 0.1925 — this model's 0.1944 is in that band, and like every published odds-free model it stays behind the closing market. The blend row is the declared experiment the brief permits (odds as a feature, never simultaneously the baseline of the same experiment): α fit on validation only. α=0.66 means the market keeps two-thirds of the weight but the model contributes a genuine third — and in-play, a pre-match-odds prior decaying with τ=30 minutes is worth 0.0034 RPS (0.14572 → 0.14235) before live events take over.

5Is it robust? Can you bet the model's pick?

Two different questions, two different answers. Both verdicts come from the same flat-stake backtest at real Bet365 prices over all 5,806 test matches.

As a forecasterROBUST

Calibration matches the bookmaker's (ECE 0.019 vs 0.018) — when the model says 60%, it happens ~60% of the time. Accuracy and RPS are stable across all nine test leagues and both test seasons, with no league collapsing. The result reproduces end-to-end from raw downloads through leakage-asserted pipelines, and the served API is bit-checked against the offline sweep.

As a betting strategyDO NOT BET

Flat-staking the v2 model's pick on every match: 53.6% of picks win, but at Bet365 prices the return is −4.7%; the α=0.66 blend's picks do no better (−4.8%) because its picks nearly coincide with the market favourite. On the earlier sweep, betting only "value picks" (model ≥3 points above market) was worse, −10.7% — the strongest possible sign that where the model disagrees with the market, the market is right and the model is wrong. Even blindly backing the market's own favourite loses −3.2%, which is simply the bookmaker's margin.

To profit you must clear the ~5–6% overround and out-forecast the closing line. The odds-free model is 0.5 RPS points behind that line, largely because closing odds price team news — confirmed injuries, actual lineups, rotation — that no historical dataset carries; the blend gets within 0.1 points but a blend of the market cannot out-price the market by enough to clear its own vig. The value flags in the app are a forecasting-error microscope, not a betting signal.

What would still move the needle (in order of cheapness)

Done in v2: shot/corner/card rolling form, validation-tuned rating parameters, and the market blend. Remaining: 1) a Dixon-Coles time-weighted goal model as a feature generator (also unlocks correct-score and over/under outputs); 2) Understat/FBref xG rolling form via the soccerdata package; 3) FC 24/25 rating refresh for the test seasons; 4) confirmed pre-kickoff lineups — the market's main informational edge, with no free leak-safe historical source. None of these change the honest expectation: the closing line is close to efficient.
