The run order, beat by beat

01Hero + three deliverables0:40
Slides: title → "Three deliverables, one question"

Say: "One question — who wins and by how much — asked at two moments: before kick-off, and again every fifth minute. Task C is H/D/A, Task R is the clipped goal margin, Task L asks both again at nineteen snapshots per match. 1,517 matches, 28,823 snapshot rows. Every classifier is scored beside the de-vigged bookmaker price on the same games."

Then one line on labels: "Labels are derived once, from the match file. Never from odds, never from events — build_label_store.py recomputes them independently and asserts agreement."

It sets the frame — market comparison — so the "we lost to the market" slide later reads as the designed result, not a disappointment. Do not explain the chips; the audience reads them.

02The pipeline forks0:45
Slide: "The pipeline forks" (ASCII diagram). Skip the two-sources slide and the split slide — fold them into this.

Say: "Two sources that share no key. StatsBomb gives events; Football-Data gives closing 1X2 odds. They are tagged together on pre-match identity only — date, home, away — with 99.71% coverage and one unmatched row logged with a reason. Notice the amber branch: it goes to evaluation and never touches the feature path. Odds are either a baseline or a feature, never both, and we chose baseline because the comparison is the framing of the whole project."

The split, in one breath: "The split is a date, not a shuffle — 896 train, 277 validation, 344 test, chronological, and validation is spent entirely on calibration so no hyperparameter ever sees it."

One diagram replaces three slides. It also pre-empts the obvious attack ("did the odds leak into your features?") before it is asked.

03The time-t cut0:45
Slide: "The time-t cut, and why it can only err safely". Skip the half-time slide — it is your best Q&A ammo.

Say: "The sharpest leakage trap is in-play. About 26 events across 24 matches carry corrupted zero timestamps that would otherwise sort to the front of their period, so we sort by (period, index) and repair the clock with a running maximum. Because accumulate only ever raises a minute, effective minute ≤ t implies true minute ≤ t. So the cut can only ever exclude too much — it can never include an event that happened after t. That is not a claim, it is a one-line proof, and assert_prefix() checks both directions on every snapshot. Seven tests, all passing, as a pipeline stage."

This is the slide that converts "we were careful" into "we proved it". Say the bolded sentence without notes.

04P2 — the sign-off gate0:45
Slide: "Hierarchical Shrinkage" filter table

Say: "P2 is Hierarchical Shrinkage — Agarwal et al., ICML 2022. Four hard filters: published 2022 or later, CORE A* venue, applicable to mixed-type tabular data at small n, and not deep learning. It passes all four. The one that actually did the work is the fifth: reimplementable from the paper text alone. DRF and GPBoost are both JMLR 2022, both excellent — they failed that filter, not the quality filter, because their approximations live in a C++ codebase and not in the paper. Hierarchical Shrinkage is one closed-form recursion with no hidden constants. FIGS is our declared fallback if the sign-off goes against it."

Examiners test whether you rejected papers for defensible reasons. Naming a rejected paper you clearly respect is stronger than naming only the winner.

05The P2 equation0:55
Slide: "Damp each split by the node it was estimated from". Skip both appendix derivation slides unless asked.
f_HS(x) = μ(t₀) + Σ_l [ μ(t_l) − μ(t_{l−1}) ] / ( 1 + λ / N(t_{l−1}) )

Say: "A leaf mean is the root mean plus the increments picked up along the path — that is an identity, it telescopes, it assumes nothing. HS damps each increment by the size of the node it was estimated from. The divisor is the parent's count, not the child's — a split taken off a large node is trusted almost fully, the identical split taken off a nearly-empty node is almost ignored. One parameter, λ."

Add the payoff, if the clock allows (10s): "Rewritten, the output is a convex combination of the ancestor means, so for classification the prediction is a valid probability vector at any λ — no clipping, no renormalising. And it is not ad-hoc: it is the exact ridge minimiser of the increments, penalised by the fraction of the parent's samples the split sent into the child."

Provenance, one sentence, mandatory: "Implemented from the paper against the raw tree_ arrays. sklearn grows the CART and nothing else. imodels was used to cross-check the scheme, never called to produce a result."

The equation plus the parent-count annotation is the single most examinable thing in the deck. If you say only one formula out loud all defence, say this one.

06What λ chose0:30cut me first
Slide: "The method diagnosed our own data"

Say: "λ is chosen by 3-fold CV inside the training rows only — tuning it on validation would turn the calibration set into a second training set. On 896 pre-match rows it picked λ=100, the top of the grid: shrink almost all the way to the base rate. On 17,024 in-play rows it picked λ=0: no shrinkage at all. Opposite ends of the same grid, on the same method, chosen without a thumb on the scale — exactly the behaviour the paper predicts. And the honest limit: HS is a variance intervention, not a bias one. It cannot repair a badly grown tree."

Turns P2 from "we implemented a paper" into "the paper told us something about our data". If you cut this beat, fold the λ=100/λ=0 contrast into one sentence at the end of beat 5.

07Task C vs the market0:45
Slide: the RPS bar chart. Skip the read-out slide; say the read-out over the chart.

Say: "Zero of seven models beat the market. The de-vigged market scores 0.19665 RPS; our best, kernel SVM, is 0.21656; P2 is 0.21659; the prior is 0.22839. The gap is plus 0.01991 RPS, and we are not softening it."

Then the methodology point: "Both are scored on the same 343 odds-tagged matches, with coverage printed beside the number — scoring the model on 344 and the market on 343 would compare two different samples. The de-vigging contract is re-asserted at the point of use, not only where the baseline was built."

Then the hypothesis, flagged as one: "Our hypothesis — offered as a hypothesis, not a finding — is that the market prices information our features do not contain: injuries, confirmed lineups, managerial news. None of that is in StatsBomb event data. We can test it; we have not, so we will not assert it."

One clause on equal budget: "Every tuned model got 12 configurations × 3 folds — random search, not grid, so the fits are equal rather than the search space, and tuning.py fails the run if any model on a task saw a different count."

The result they will probe hardest. Leading with the loss, unhedged, and following with the same-rows argument is what makes it a result rather than an excuse.

08In-play curve + close0:55
Slides: RPS-by-minute chart → "Four findings" (close on it)

Say: "The dashed amber line is the pre-match prediction, frozen and repeated across all 19 snapshots — flat by construction, and the code asserts it is flat, because any slope would mean the reference leaked match progress. The in-play model does not cross it until minute 15. For the first sixth of a match, knowing who is playing beats knowing what has happened. That is a statement about football, not about our code."

The endpoint caveat — say it before anyone asks: "Do not misread the right-hand end. RPS 0.024 at minute 90 is the task definition asserting itself, not model skill. We measured a falling curve with a crossover at 10 to 15 minutes; that is all we will say about it."

Close: "One more finding worth 10 seconds: aggregate calibration lies on this data. Every model is over-confident for 75 minutes and under-confident for the last 15, and a single ECE averages those two errors against each other into a small, reassuring number. That is why the per-phase table exists. Still to build: SHAP, and converting Task R's margin into H/D/A probabilities. P2 is presented for sign-off — not presented as approved."

Ends on the honest reading, which is the tone the brief rewards, and ends with the explicit ask (sign-off), which is the actual purpose of the session.

Defending every choice — one line each

The pattern: choice → the alternative → why the alternative is worse. Never defend a choice by saying it is standard.

ChoiceThe defence
Chronological split, not shuffleA shuffled split lets the model learn February from March and be scored on January. The cost is real and we accept it: the test window is the March–May run-in, when title and relegation races distort behaviour and the bookmaker is sharpest. We did not pick an easy quarter.
Validation spent on calibrationIf hyperparameters and the calibrator are fit on the same rows, every reported ECE is optimistic. So tuning CV runs inside train only, and validation is untouched until the calibrator.
Odds as baseline, not featureThe brief permits either, never both in one experiment. The model-versus-market comparison is the framing of the project, so a feature-arm would cost us the headline.
shift(1) before rolling(5)Roll-then-shift silently includes the match being predicted. We assert every team's first match of the season is NaN — a non-NaN there would mean the window reached into the present.
Sort by (period, index), not timestamp~26 events across 24 matches carry corrupted 00:00:00 timestamps that would sort to the front of their period. The code is right; REPORT.md §3 is stale prose and is on the TODO list. Volunteer this before they find it.
Half-time events pinned behind 45′StatsBomb's minute is not globally monotonic — period 1 runs to 47′, period 2 restarts at 45′. Of the two ways to be wrong at the boundary we chose the one that drops information over the one that invents it. Capping period 1 at 45′ would pull stoppage-time events into the t=45 snapshot — that is the direction that leaks.
Random search, not gridA grid would hand LightGBM 729 configurations and kernel SVM 20. Fixing the number of fits is the only thing "comparable budget" can honestly mean. 12 × 3 = 36 fits per tuned model per task, asserted.
λ not in the outer searchGiving HS an outer λ grid on top of the paper's inner CV would hand it a larger budget than every other model. λ is chosen the paper's way, inside train.
No kernels on Task LNot a gap — a measured decision. The theoretical Gram at 17,024 rows is 2,211 MB; exact kernel ridge fits as t ~ n^2.245, Nyström as n^0.392. We report the curve as its own deliverable, and the exponents are asserted so the script fails if the exact methods stop looking super-linear.
Kernel ridge capped at 8,000 rowsDeclared per row: the results table carries a subsampled flag and the real n_train, so the compute row cannot be misread as a full-data fit. Note Nyström beats it on both MAE and RMSE — the subsampling costs more than the approximation.
PROBABILITY_FLOOR = 0.005Isotonic on a ~340-row validation split emits exact zeros. RPS and Brier barely notice; log-loss exploded to 1.9–2.8 nats and made every model look far worse than the market for a reason that was purely an artefact. Post-floor it lands at 1.05–1.22. We report the fix rather than hide it.
P2 as a forest in the results tableBoth HSTreeClassifier and HSForestClassifier exist. The single tree is the paper's interpretability argument; the forest goes in the table because the comparison set is ensembles and a lone depth-limited CART would lose for reasons unrelated to HS.
These four leagues, this seasonA rule, not a preference, implemented in competition_audit.py: partial or single-club releases are excluded because opponent-side form would be undefined; women's and tournament data is excluded because it cannot be joined to a bookmaker, and a test set that cannot sit beside the market is meaningless here.
Frozen pre-match as the Task L baselineBoth baselines are reported. The prior is the sanity floor; the frozen curve is the bar that justifies Model 3 existing at all. A model that merely matches it has learned nothing from the live stream, however good its absolute RPS looks.
P1 over SMOTENeither is "the winner". SMOTE takes the best draw recall (0.231) and F1 (0.243) at the worst RPS (0.223). P1 takes the best RPS (0.219) and best calibration (ECE 0.045) by finding fewer draws. They optimise different things; our headline metric is RPS, so P1 wins the arm we run.

The two questions the brief names — full answers, memorise the shape

Snapshots every minute instead of every five?
Rows go 19 → 91 per match, 28,823 → ~138,000, but information barely moves: state variables only change on discrete events (~2.7 goals, ~4 cards a match), so adjacent snapshots differ only in the rolling recent-rate features and their autocorrelation approaches 1. Nominal n rises 4.8×; effective sample size does not. Consequences: ungrouped CV becomes more optimistic so GroupKFold matters more not less; naive standard errors are optimistic by roughly √(1+(m−1)ρ); compute rises ~5× for trees and ~23× for anything with a Gram matrix. The leakage machinery is unchanged — prefix_length_at is defined for any t. What we defend: the 5-minute grid wins on cost per unit information, and the right way to buy resolution is event-triggered snapshots — at each goal, red card and substitution — not a finer uniform grid.
What if SMOTE were applied to the snapshot table?
It breaks three ways, which is why the code raises rather than warns. (1) The synthetic rows are not game states — interpolating (min 40, 1–0, 11v11) with (min 75, 0–2, 10v11) yields minute 57.5, goal difference −0.4, 10.6 players; goal difference and man-advantage are integers, cumulative xG is monotone in minute. (2) It moves match-level label information across the match boundary — a synthetic row inherits match A's eventual result while half its feature mass comes from match B. (3) It inflates n while shrinking effective sample size, so a few matches dominate training and every internal CV score gets more optimistic while the model gets worse. Also snapshot_minute is itself a feature, so interpolation produces off-grid minutes and makes the per-phase analysis incoherent. Enforced structurally: prepare_matrices raises a ValueError on any resampling arm over a frame containing snapshot_minute. If forced to rebalance anyway: class_weight='balanced', resample within a match or phase, or resample whole matches carrying all 19 snapshots together.

Rapid-fire Q&A — one or two sentences each

Isn't 0.02 RPS basically the same as the market?
No, and we will not call it close. On RPS at this scale that is the difference between the prior and a working model — the prior is 0.228, the market 0.197, our best 0.217, so the market's edge is over 60% of the total signal any of our models found.
Eight models within 0.05 MAE — did anything work?
That flatness is the result. Eight very different learners on identical budgets landing within 0.05 goals of each other, all ~7% better than the prior, is the signal ceiling of pre-match features measured eight independent ways. Pre-match goal margin is close to irreducibly noisy.
How do you know your HS is really the paper's HS?
A second, deliberately literal implementation transcribed from the PDF — walking each root-to-leaf path node by node — agrees with the fast recursion. Plus λ=0 ≡ plain CART, λ→∞ ≡ the root mean, and simplex closure at every λ. Eight tests, run as a pipeline stage, so a run that breaks the reimplementation fails the pipeline.
Why is your log-loss so different from earlier numbers?
The probability floor. Isotonic on ~340 validation rows emits exact zeros; log-loss is the only metric that notices violently. We report before and after because a number that moves 1.5 nats on a floor is one you must understand before presenting it.
Your suite is short one booster in an earlier table.
Known and now pinned: model_zoo.py guards the xgboost and lightgbm imports in try/except, so without libomp they vanish with no warning and the zoo silently returns 4 of 6. Documented, and the current table is complete.
What is missing?
SHAP is not written — TreeSHAP over the already-persisted predictions_*.csv, no refitting, shap==0.52.0 pinned. Converting Task R's margin into H/D/A probabilities is required by the brief and not attempted; it is an analysis over two prediction files on the same test matches, not new modelling. REPORT.md ends at §3 of §9.

Discipline

Say these without notes

  • The four hard filters — and that DRF and GPBoost failed the fifth
  • The HS equation, and that the damping uses the parent's count
  • "The cut can only ever exclude too much"
  • "Zero of seven models beat the market. The gap is 0.01991 RPS."
  • "P2 is presented for sign-off, not presented as approved."

Never say

  • That any model beat the market
  • That P2 is approved
  • That Task L is "real time"
  • That the model "converges to certainty" at minute 90
  • That you implemented HS "from imodels" — from the paper, against raw tree_ arrays
  • "We will improve accuracy." Say what you will build and what it will show
  • Anything hedging the market loss — "close", "nearly", "essentially matched"
